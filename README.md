# YTV

Watch YouTube like TV: your own channels keep playing, full screen, with a swipeable list of what's next. For Chrome and Brave. 100% local: no accounts and no servers.

YTV used to be Hush TV, inside Hush for YouTube. It's now its own extension; Hush keeps tidying YouTube's pages and YTV does the watching. They work well together, and each works on its own.

## Install (Chrome or Brave)

1. Unzip this folder somewhere permanent, such as `Documents/ytv`. The browser loads the extension from it, so don't delete it.
2. Open `chrome://extensions` (or `brave://extensions`) and turn on **Developer mode** (top right).
3. Click **Load unpacked** and choose the `ytv` folder (the one with `manifest.json`).
4. Pin YTV from the puzzle-piece menu.

**Coming from Hush TV?** Update Hush to 5.0 too. Your channels and TV settings come over by themselves the next time you open YouTube. What you've watched, your Feed and your progress carry over as well.

## Using it

- **Start:** the YTV button in YouTube's top bar (with a menu of your channels), the YTV entry in the sidebar, or the toolbar menu.
- **Channels:** your **Feed** (channel 0, your own list), **Home** (the same videos as your YouTube home page, in YouTube's order or shuffled), Subscriptions, **Watch Later** (your whole list and nothing else: it stops at the end), **Twitch** (optional: whoever of your streamers is live, in Twitch's own player), and your own channels mixing topics, creators and your home feed.
- **Keys:** **,** and **.** move through the list's tabs (just looking), **Shift+,** and **Shift+.** go to the next / previous tab and play its first video (these take over YouTube's speed keys while YTV is on), **Shift+[** and **Shift+]** go back and forth through everything you watched, across all channels and Twitch, **[** and **]** to the previous / next video, **U** opens the list, **H** switches how the list hides, **`** turns YTV on or off, **E** explores, **Shift+E** searches YouTube (all changeable).
- **Top bar:** rounded blocks (default), one solid bar, or a soft fade into the video, set in Look & feel. YouTube's own title is hidden underneath. Left: the title, with the creator, length and date beneath. Right: back, info, comments and **⋯**, which holds forward, explore, add or hide this creator, Watch Later, and on a stream the quality (with their keys). The channel overview is **G**.
- **Play next stays put:** videos you put up next yourself (Shift-swipe, Shift+A, Play next, or dragged below what's playing) keep their place when you refresh the list.
- **Twitch:** who's live first (how many watch on each picture; point at one for its ⓘ info button), with who's offline greyed and folded away under one "Offline" bar between them and the live ones (click it to show them); **E** on the Twitch tab shows what's live in a game or category (click a stream to watch it, + to add them); **Shift+E** or the magnifier finds streamers by name. **I** on a stream has a button to add them to Twitch or take them off, in your order (drag to change it, or pick "most watched first"). Click a live one to play it; point at one a moment first and it's already playing out of sight, so the click is instant. Nothing in the list moves when you switch. YTV's helper inside Twitch's player (only when YTV shows it on YouTube) gets past the "start watching" and Play buttons by itself, turns the sound on, picks the quality you chose (the gear in the top bar, or settings; Twitch only gives logged-in viewers the higher qualities), keeps a click on the picture from pausing it (**Space** or **K** pauses and plays), and hides Follow, Gift a Sub, Subscribe and "Watch on Twitch". Twitch's own play/pause, mute and volume (bottom left in the player) work as usual and stay as you set them; M mutes and unmutes, double-click is YTV's full screen, and every YTV key works over the stream too. The list keeps itself up to date: who's live, titles, how many watch and the preview pictures are asked again every 90 seconds. **I** shows the title, how long they've been live and how many watch, with their **past broadcasts** under it to watch right here (click someone offline for theirs). The chat starts with what was said just before you tuned in. Over the video on dark bars (point at it for its handle: drag it anywhere, resize it from the corner, A− A+ and ◐ for this streamer; there it keeps every message), or in the list panel (the panel goes see-through, the messages keep their dark bars; A− A+ and ◐ in its corner) — one at a time. **O** shows and hides the chat where you keep it (over the video by default), **Shift+O** moves it to the other place; when it shows again it starts with what was said so far. The chat is only connected while you can see it: hide it and the connection closes, show it again and it reconnects with what was just said. Read only, with BetterTTV, FrankerFaceZ and 7TV emotes. Pick the streamers you want a notification about when they go live (also while YouTube is closed). Find streamers by name in settings. After a reload (on a video page or any other YouTube page) the stream comes straight back, and YouTube's own video never plays behind it. (Twitch extensions such as Hearthstone Deck Tracker don't load in Twitch's embedded player, so their pop-ups can't show here.)
- **As a Mac app:** settings → On YouTube → **Set up the app** opens YouTube with YTV's app link and shows what to click (Chrome or Brave). YTV then starts by itself when you open it from the Dock. `https://www.youtube.com/?ytvapp` also works as a bookmark.
- **Full screen:** when YouTube puts its controls up by itself (nobody moved the mouse), YTV and YouTube's controls stay hidden, and YouTube's own full-screen title never shows under YTV's.
- **Search (E or Shift+E):** a plain search box: type and press Enter, and YouTube's results open as a tab in the list. Under it, the topics of what's playing (or of the video you right-clicked → Search from this video): click one to play only that topic until you go back (B). It pops up with a short animation. The 🔍 on a YouTube video's picture is always there.
- **Comments (O):** new comments come in one after another, top to bottom; pull past the end for more (as in the list).
- **Settings** open where you left off (the page and how far down it); Esc closes them.
- **Channels in a search:** the channels that match show at the top of the results tab; click one for its page in Info (their Latest and Popular videos).
- **Twitch, someone offline:** click them (in the list, the ∨ menu or Find streamers) for their page in Info: past broadcasts, and an Add to Twitch button that turns into a small bin once they're on your list.
- **Skip ads (Settings → Playing, on by default):** YouTube's own Skip button on an ad is clicked the moment it shows.
- **Reloading the page:** the video carries on where you were from the very first frame (no jump in picture or sound a moment later), in the quality you picked.
- **More from … (right-click any video):** Info for that video with its creator's Latest and Popular videos, and a Play now button.
- **Info (I):** the description (a long one starts folded to a few lines: Show more, which opens and folds smoothly), with the creator, date, length, the channel's subscriber count and the video's views, each with its small icon.
- **History (H, or the clock above the list):** everything you watched, newest first, across channels and Twitch, kept after a restart. Click one to watch it again; what's on now is marked. **Shift+[** and **Shift+]** step through it too. It's a list like the others: swipe one to add it to your Feed (Shift: play next) or forget it.
- **Continue watching** is a channel (right after your Feed until you move it): YouTube videos you started and didn't finish (also ones you half watched on YouTube itself), newest first, each from where you were. It plays like any channel and stops at its end. Not Twitch.
- **Watched:** what you already watched on a channel is folded away under one "Watched" bar right above what's playing; click it to show them (they fade in and out quickly, like Offline on Twitch). Clicking the video closes the list only when you opened it with its key (U); opened from the screen edge, a click pauses as usual. Switch the bar off in settings (List → Rows) and they stay in view, greyed out. History keeps the last 200 by default (also there).
- **The list's header:** the funnel, History and refresh (and on Twitch the magnifier) at the top right. Filters that are on show as one pill ("2 filters · Clear").
- **Feedback** stays on the row: a short flash when something worked, a small shake when that way isn't possible.
- **Settings** open right over TV (from the channel overview), so full screen stays as it is; ✕ or Esc closes them.
- **Hiding:** the setting in List (a key for it can be set under Keyboard) switches between hiding the list whenever the mouse rests, only in full screen, or never. Point at the right edge of the screen (between the top bar and YouTube's controls) and the list slides out, and the small tab just outside its left edge closes it again; it goes again a moment after the mouse is back over the video (not when you overshoot to the browser's tabs).
- **Icons:** Feed and the ready-made channels show as icons in the tab bar, and small icons sit before the creator, date and length (list and top bar each switchable). The title has its own size; the rest of its line is a little smaller.
- **The list (U):** its tabs follow your channel order (as in the overview, G; dragging a tab moves the channel). **H**, **I** and **O** open History, Info or Comments; pressed again they take you back to the list (if you came from it) or close the panel (if you were just watching). When the panel hides by itself, it comes back as the list. **S** switches sorting off and on: off, the list stays exactly as it is however much you switch (nothing greys, folds, moves or goes, and it doesn't scroll to what's playing); back on, it counts from that moment: nothing that already happened changes, what's playing moves to the top (under the Watched bar, which keeps what it had), videos you watched with sorting off stay in view, and from then on each video you leave goes into the Watched bar. With sorting off the Watched bar stays too, holding what it held, and everything above it looks watched (the same greyed style); videos you play don't grey. Drag a video above or below the bar, or drag the bar itself, to decide what counts as watched. With sorting on, the bar is stuck to what's playing: dragging it drags that video. The bar is always there, also before you've watched anything. Each tab remembers where you scrolled; the first time, what's playing sits at the top with what you watched just above it, out of view. It fades away together with YouTube's player controls when the mouse rests (switchable), never while you point at it. Compact tabs that stay where they are: a new one opens on the right, drag one sideways to move it, a middle click closes it, and/or an ✕ on each tab (both in settings → List → Tabs), and **+** adds a channel as a tab. The ▾ tab for looking at any channel is optional (List & swiping): Feed · channels you pinned · the channel playing · the channel you're looking at (▾ picks any) · topics you're exploring · **+** pins a channel as a tab.
- **Explore (E):** pick a topic from the video, or Cmd-click several to explore them together (or type them with commas). Hashtags find their videos too. Typing there never reaches the video, and the video behind is softly blurred.
- **Search (Shift+E):** search YouTube for videos; the results open as a tab in the list, where you swipe one to add it to your Feed or click one to watch it.
- **Several topics at once:** exploring a second topic keeps the first as its own tab with what was left of it. Pick a video in a waiting topic to carry on with that one.
- **How a channel is put together:** each source keeps YouTube's own ranking (no reshuffling unless the channel is set to shuffle), the same creator never plays twice in a row when there's anyone else, and videos you mostly watched already (here or on youtube.com) go to the end. When you watch a video to the end, a couple of YouTube's own suggestions after it join the list a little further down, if they fit the channel (its creators or topics). All three can be switched off under Playing → What plays.
- **Info (I) shows the creator's videos:** under the description, *Latest* or *Popular* (as on their channel), loading more as you scroll down. Click one and it plays right away (as next, so your channel carries on after it); swipe, A / D and right-click work as in the list.
- **The ∨ grid (V):** the open list (or, with no list open, the list of what's playing) as a big grid of videos over the video, in and out of full screen: press **V**, scroll down on the video (also over a Twitch stream), or click the ∨ at the bottom. It slides up from the bottom and back down (easing in and out; its speed is a setting). Scrolling down on the video, the grid follows you; past half (or with a quick swipe) it snaps open, otherwise it slides back when you let go. At its top, a swipe up pulls it away the same way. A scroll that runs into its top only stops there (one small overshoot); the rest of that scroll is dropped and the next swipe pulls. How easily is a setting (Playing): easily, normal, firm, or never, and so are how far you have to pull it (as a % of the screen) and how quickly it snaps. Resting the pointer at the very top of the open grid closes it, and so does leaving the screen over the top; the zone glows red like the screen edge that brings the list out (how much of the screen counts is a setting; it never opens the grid). Opened again on the same list, the grid is where you left it. A click plays the video; V, Esc, the ∧ or a scroll up at the top closes it. Swipe a tile sideways with two fingers — the very same swipe as a row in the list (it's the same code): the plus (Shift: plays next) or the bin, on the same sides, with the same threshold and colours; **A**, **Shift+A** and **D** do it on the tile under the pointer, and Cmd+Z undoes it. (Twitch streams don't swipe, as in the list.) Set how many per row under Playing (smaller tiles, more at once). It takes the place of YouTube's own full-screen grid. Its background stays full screen and only fades; the grid itself slides. At its top: the list's tabs as chips (pick one to see that list; they follow the list menu and it follows them). It has its own page in settings, where you can also switch it off; the list menu on the right can be switched off too (its tabs then live in the ∨ menu, and U opens that). Its tabs stay at the top while you scroll (the top hot zone leaves them alone), and the tab keys (, and .) switch its tabs while it's open. Videos fade in as they load, like the list. It comes up from halfway down the screen, fading in. Only one at a time: opening the ∨ menu closes the list panel and the overview (G), and opening either of those closes the ∨ menu. Its bar sits at the very top on a soft blurred fade: the tabs (and + to pin a channel) in the middle, refresh and ∧ on the right. Offline streamers are greyed out; live ones show how many watch with an eye. It reacts to a scroll at once, showing up as soon as you start. A right-click on a video opens the same menu as in the list (play now or next, Feed, Watch Later, explore, hide the creator, remove; Shift + right-click: the browser's own). - **Twitch:** a click on the stream closes the list (opened with its key), as on YouTube.

## Settings

Every block has **Reset to default**, settings you changed are marked, and the preview next to the page (list, top bar, colours, overview and Twitch pages) follows every change while you drag, lighting up the part of the screen the block you point at changes. Sizes have no small maximums any more (text, tabs, rounding, blur, chat and so on go as large as you like).

- **Channels:** *Your channels* (topics, creators with YouTube search and "Similar", hidden creators, filters; each folds open), *Ready-made channels* (Home and its order, Subscriptions, Watch Later) and *Twitch* (Twitch on / off, find and add streamers, order, quality, offline streamers, where O puts the chat, the chat over the video and in the panel, emotes, go-live notifications).
- **Watching:** *List* (when it hides, the right edge, tabs: middle-click and ✕ each on their own, rows, row text, the buttons on a picture), *Swiping* (a live demo, direction, distance, colours, the keyboard keys), *Top bar*, *Colours & style* (themes, colours, list panel, size, animations), *Channel overview*, *Playing* (skip music, buttons above the player bar) and *Keyboard* (your keys, plus the fixed ones).
- **YTV:** *On YouTube* (top bar and sidebar buttons), a search box at the top (Cmd/Ctrl+F) that finds any setting, *Health check* (tries everything YTV reads from YouTube and makes a report to send when something breaks), *Backup* (export / import, also takes a Hush settings file) and *About*.

## Privacy and security

- Permissions: only `storage`, and only on `www.youtube.com`, plus Twitch's player (`player.twitch.tv`) for the Twitch helper, which does nothing unless YouTube embeds the player. Twitch asks your browser for `gql.twitch.tv` only when you switch it on; YTV then only asks Twitch which of your streamers are live (no cookies sent). The chat over a stream is an anonymous, read-only connection to Twitch's chat (nothing can be sent from it), and the extra emotes come from the public lists of BetterTTV, FrankerFaceZ and 7TV.
- Apart from that, YTV only talks to youtube.com, through two functions in `src/yt-data.js` that refuse any other address: reading YouTube pages, and YouTube's own API for comments, the next page of a list, and your Watch Later list. Your Watch Later list only changes when you ask.
- No remote or dynamic code, no `innerHTML`, no analytics. Messages are only accepted from YTV itself.

## Developing

YTV runs as one file, `src/ytv.js`, but is edited as parts in `dev/tv/` (`01-data.js` … `07-start.js`, plus `tv.css`). After changing a part, run `node dev/build.js`. Never edit `src/ytv.js` by hand.

`tests/tv/` has browser tests on a mock YouTube page (Node, Playwright and Chromium): start `python3 tests/tv/serve.py`, then `node tests/tv/run.js` (YTV itself) and `node tests/tv/pages.js` (settings and toolbar menu) and `node tests/tv/soak.js` (opens and closes YTV many times and checks nothing piles up). `node tests/tv/stress.js` is heavy use (a chat flooding at 300 messages a second, 30 stream switches, YTV opened and closed on Twitch, a list of hundreds with Load more): smooth, nothing left behind, memory flat. `node tests/tv/e2e-twitch.js` loads the real extension with stand-ins for YouTube, Twitch's player and Twitch's chat (it needs a self-signed certificate for irc-ws.chat.twitch.tv in `E2E_DIR`).
